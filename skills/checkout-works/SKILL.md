# Check-out works

Read the request, check-out inspection report and any estimate/invoice available now.
Build a per-item scope: cleaning, decoration, damaged fittings, flooring and other
requested work. Separate tenant damage claims from normal maintenance; don't infer
liability or deposit deductions from an invoice alone.

Check existing assignments and landlord instructions before soliciting quotes. Where
the landlord requests a comparison, obtain the additional estimate and present a
like-for-like comparison of scope, VAT, exclusions and timing. Clarify conflicting
names, descriptions and attachments rather than treating an inspection report as a
quote. Avoid booking duplicate work or repeating already received estimates.

Send the quote and relevant inspection evidence to the landlord in the existing thread.
Wait for documented approval of the scope and spending before instructing the selected
provider. If the landlord has already approved the exact work, continue from that point.
Confirm vacant-property access, key collection and a real attendance window. Chase
unanswered quote/approval requests on a planned deadline; record holds and wake-ups.

After work, reconcile each requested item against the completion report, photos if
required and the invoice. Costs may decrease when a requested replacement isn't needed;
document the explanation. A competing outstanding invoice requires clarification, not
an invented payment. Use contractor-invoice to distinguish charges, approval for payment
and actual settlement. A landlord paying directly is not an agency payment.

Record the outcome, missing evidence and return of keys; notify the landlord/tenant as
appropriate. Close only after the applicable checks pass. Preserve original source
documents and event references in the ticket's final_answer and reasoning.
